# How to Read This Repo

*A short guide.*

---

## The two things that get conflated

Throughout the repo, two kinds of content appear:

1. **The generator and its strict grammar** — the dialectical method (in [`02-the-process/`](../02-the-process/)) and the standing properties of the totality it forces from the three postulates (in [`03-the-grammar/`](../03-the-grammar/)). This is the framework itself.
2. **The experiments** — specific applications of the generator to specific domains, each adding additional commitments beyond the postulates. These live in [`10-experiments/`](../10-experiments/).

Keep them separate. A wrong experiment does not invalidate the generator. A successful experiment is encouraging evidence but not definitive.

## How to evaluate any step

For any move in any document:

- Identify the prior commitments (postulates only, or postulates + experiment-level commitments).
- Try to construct an alternative move that satisfies them.
- If no alternative works, the move is **forced**.
- If alternatives exist and the document selects one with stated reasons, the move is **chosen**, and the selecting principle is itself a commitment.
- If alternatives exist and no reason is stated, flag it as missing justification.

Detail in [`02-the-process/forced-vs-chosen.md`](../02-the-process/forced-vs-chosen.md).

## Vocabulary for rigor

When reading or writing, use these labels:

- **Theorem** — formal proof from prior content (e.g., consequences of standard algebra applied to a chosen equation).
- **Construction** — follows from prior content under the framework's paraconsistent dialectical logic; not a Hilbert-style proof.
- **Observation** — structural claim defended by reasoning; not formally derived.
- **Commitment** — a chosen move beyond the postulates, with stated reasons. An experiment is a chain of commitments plus consequences.
- **Assignment** — a specific identification within an experiment (this domain element corresponds to that framework structure). A revisable hypothesis.

Default to *Construction* when in doubt; look for explicit framing.

## On numerical claims

Quantitative claims live in [`10-experiments/`](../10-experiments/). When evaluating:

- Identify the experiment's commitments.
- Identify the structural inputs the formula uses.
- Check whether inputs were assigned *independently* of the output, or *after* seeing the target.
- Check the precision of any agreement.

Inputs assigned independently → *prediction*. Inputs chosen to match output → *fit*. Both inform; only predictions are evidence the framework is generative beyond data already in hand.

## Possible experiment outcomes

Each experiment is a hypothesis: *applying the generator with these commitments to this domain produces these results.* Four outcomes:

- **Succeed cleanly** — predictions match without strain.
- **Succeed with strain** — predictions match but assignments needed adjustment.
- **Fail** — no construction under the commitments matches the domain.
- **Be silent** — the commitments don't produce predictions for the phenomenon.

Only the third refutes — and only *that experiment*, not the framework.

## A note on tone

You will encounter language that sounds metaphysical: *void, totality, witnessing, internality, polarity*. These are technical terms — they name structural positions. If a passage feels poetic, look for the structural content underneath. It should be there. If it isn't, the passage is decoration and should be flagged.
