# Cross-Domain Analysis

*The framework's research program at the meta-experimental level.*

---

## What this is

Each [experiment](../10-experiments/) is the generator articulating itself through a particular lens — through the slice of the totality that lens engages. Cross-domain analysis examines what these self-articulations reveal *in common*: which features of the generator show up regardless of which lens is used, and which features are specific to particular lenses.

This is not the framework being "applied" to domains as if to external objects. By [Internality](../01-postulates/internality.md), there are no external objects. Every domain is internal to the totality. The generator articulating through a domain is the totality articulating itself in the form that domain provides. Cross-domain analysis is therefore self-portraiture across lenses, with the goal of finding what is invariant.

## What becomes visible only here

Three things become visible across multiple experiments that no single experiment can show:

1. **Hidden structure in the strict grammar.** If every successful experiment, regardless of its specific commitments, requires some structural feature, the postulates may force more than the [grammar](../03-the-grammar/) currently articulates. Cross-domain regularity is the empirical signal we missed something.

2. **Promotion candidates.** A commitment that recurs across many independent experiments is a candidate for promotion from experiment-level to framework-level. Cross-domain ubiquity is the test of structural significance.

3. **The generator's natural reach.** Mapping where the framework consistently produces hits, where it produces silences, and where it fails maps the generator's actual domain of applicability. Some lenses turn out to be the generator's natural home; others may resist articulation in ways that suggest different commitments are needed — or that those phenomena aren't structural in the framework's sense.

## The trap to avoid: false convergence

The most common failure in cross-domain analysis is finding convergence that isn't real. Three sources:

- **Vocabulary convergence.** Experiments use the same word ("polarity," "recursion") with different structural meanings. Lexical match is not structural match.
- **Self-selection.** The experimenters share commitments (often implicit) that bias all experiments toward the same kind of structure. The convergence is in the experimenters' minds, not in the totality.
- **Confirmation bias.** Looking for what one wants to find and stopping when found.

The discipline for distinguishing real from false convergence is in [`methodology.md`](methodology.md).
