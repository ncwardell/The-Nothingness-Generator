# Cross-Domain Transferability

*Forced atoms are forced atoms of the generator. They appear wherever the generator appears.*

---

## The principle

> **A relationship that is *forced* in one experiment — i.e., follows from the postulates plus that experiment's commitments without further choice — is a structural fact about the generator. It must be articulable in any other experiment whose lens engages the same structural feature, in that experiment's own vocabulary.**

This is the positive complement to the [false-convergence trap](methodology.md). Where false convergence warns "patterns may be artifacts of vocabulary or self-selection," transferability says: **patterns that are genuinely forced *should* show up wherever the structure is engaged**. Failure to transfer is itself information.

## Why this is forced

By the lens-reframe (see [`10-experiments/README.md`](../10-experiments/README.md)), every experiment is the generator articulating itself through a particular lens. The generator is the same across lenses; only the articulation form differs. Therefore:

- A *forced* result in lens A is the generator's structural truth, articulated through A's vocabulary.
- That same structural truth must be articulable through any other lens that engages the same part of the generator — in that lens's vocabulary, with that lens's commitments.
- The articulation will *look different* (different vocabulary, different formal expression) but the *structure* is the same.

Forced facts are not lens-specific. They are generator-facts seen through a lens.

## What this gives us

Three uses:

### 1. Structural prediction

A discovery in one experiment predicts findings in another. If experiment A (mathematics) forces $\phi\psi = -1$ as a Vieta consequence, and experiment B (a structurally rich domain) engages the same self-reference structure, B should articulate the same relationship in B's vocabulary. We can *look for it before completing B's independent unfolding* — a guided search.

### 2. Consistency check

If a forced fact in A cannot be found in B, and B genuinely engages the same structural feature, something is off. Possibilities:

- A's claim wasn't actually forced; some commitment was hidden.
- B's articulation is incomplete; the fact is there but not yet visible.
- B's lens doesn't actually engage the feature, despite appearance.
- Genuine breakdown — revealing a structural feature we don't yet understand.

Each is informative.

### 3. Test of structural-vs-lens-specific

A result that transfers across multiple lenses with different commitments is structural. A result that doesn't transfer (where transfer is expected) is lens-specific — a feature of the chosen articulation, not of the generator.

This is how the [`candidate-refinements.md`](candidate-refinements.md) gets populated: a result that consistently transfers across many lenses is a candidate for promotion to framework-level.

## The condition: lens engagement

Transferability requires that **the receiving lens engage the structural feature in question**. If lens B has no way to express dynamic re-enactment, then a Fibonacci-type result from lens A has nothing to transfer to. That's not a failure of transferability — it's evidence that B engages a different part of the generator.

So the methodology is:

1. **Identify the structural feature** the forced result in A engages (which postulate, which standing property, which operating principle).
2. **For each other experiment B**, ask: does B engage that feature?
3. If yes, look for the articulation of the same result in B's vocabulary.
4. Document hits, misses, and silences.

Hits across multiple Bs strengthen the case that the result is structural. Misses (where engagement is genuine) reveal hidden commitments or incomplete articulations. Silences (where engagement is absent) map the boundary of the feature.

## What this does *not* license

Transferability is a discipline about *forced* results. It does not license:

- Transferring chosen commitments. A commitment in A (e.g., a specific algebraic form) does not transfer to B unless B independently makes the same commitment.
- Transferring assignments. A specific assignment in A (e.g., "particle X corresponds to position Y") is lens-specific; it does not transfer.
- Transferring numerical values that depend on lens-specific commitments. A number in A might require A's specific commitment about, say, the unit of measurement.

What transfers is the **structural relationship** — the form, not the contents. If A finds that two structural quantities related by self-reference satisfy a particular Vieta-like identity, B should find the analogous Vieta-like identity in B's vocabulary, but the specific numerical values (if any) will depend on B's own commitments.

## The dialectic with false convergence

Two principles work in tension:

- **False convergence** warns: don't confuse vocabulary or self-selection for structure.
- **Transferability** asserts: forced structure must transfer.

The discipline is to apply both:

- A claimed cross-domain pattern must survive false-convergence audit (different commitment chains, different experimenters, different vocabulary).
- A forced result must satisfy transferability (it appears wherever the structure is engaged).

A pattern that satisfies both is strong evidence the result is structural. A pattern that fails false-convergence audit is artifact. A forced result that fails transferability indicates either the result wasn't actually forced or the receiving lens has a hidden non-engagement of the feature.

## The witness requirement

A sharper structural prediction than generic transferability: **every lens in which the framework genuinely closes must surface a witness-analog — something internal to the lens that plays the registration role that constitutes polarity as distinction.**

This follows from [Self-Reference as distinguishment](../03-the-grammar/03-self-reference.md#self-reference-as-distinguishment): the polar pair *is* a distinction only if something within the totality holds it as distinct. Any lens that closes must therefore contain its own articulation of that holding.

Sketches of what the witness-analog looks like in each lens:

| Lens | Witness-analog |
|---|---|
| **Mathematics** | the variable referencing itself in the self-reference equation |
| **Language** | the speaker / sentence relation; the act of articulation |
| **Physics** | measurement; the act of registration that determines a basis |
| **Biology** | the cell registering its own boundary; metabolism as self-distinction |
| **Awareness / phenomenology** | attention itself; the holding from within |

This is a stronger test than the generic transferability principle. A lens that closes without surfacing a witness-analog has either smuggled the witness in implicitly (a hidden commitment to be flagged) or is closing on something other than the framework's seed (a false-convergence candidate). A lens that *cannot* surface a witness-analog is one the framework's discipline says it shouldn't close in — a real structural signal, not a "miss" to be reclassified.

The witness requirement is therefore both a guide for designing experiments and a check for auditing them. It is the sharpest empirical prediction the framework makes at the cross-domain level.

## Practical workflow

For each forced result in any experiment:

1. **Tag it** with the structural feature(s) it engages.
2. **Predict** which other experiments should articulate it.
3. **Look** for it in those experiments' independent unfoldings.
4. **Document** in [`observations.md`](observations.md): predicted, found / not-found / pending, with the strength-of-evidence.

Over time, this creates a map of which structural features are tightly forced (high transferability) vs which are partly lens-specific (weaker transferability). That map is the framework's empirical content.

## The Algebraic Package as Canonical Structural Template

The strongest form of transferability is structural overlay: **every successful lens has the same structural shape as the algebraic package, with different content filling each position.**

This follows from the [Universal Bootstrap Principle](../02-the-process/self-bootstrap.md#the-universal-bootstrap-principle): every lens has a substrate plus an articulation, and the articulation is "algebra" in the universal sense (a language for structural relations). Since the algebraic package (arithmetic + algebra) is a complete bootstrap-verified instance of this, it serves as the **canonical structural template** for any other lens.

Numbers and operations are not exclusively mathematical objects. They are **structural positions** that any lens fills with its own content. The number "1" is shorthand for "the unit of distinction in the lens." The structure is universal; the content is lens-specific.

### The template positions

Every lens that bootstraps fills these positions:

| Position | What it represents |
|---|---|
| **Polar pair** | the lens's fundamental opposition |
| **Unit of distinction (1)** | the lens's basic atomic distinguishable element |
| **Zero** | the lens's conservative reference / null state |
| **Negation map** | the lens's polarity-reversing operation |
| **Addition / subtraction** | the lens's combining / decomposing operation pair |
| **Iteration / time** | the lens's repetition / temporal extension |
| **Multiplication / division** | second-order combining / decomposing |
| **Fibonacci-analog** | the lens's minimal self-referential dynamic |
| **Self-reference equation** | the lens's algebraic articulation of self-reference |
| **Two roots (φ, ψ)** | the lens's eigenvalues of self-reference |
| **Vieta / degenerate triple** | the lens's algebraic regeneration of Conservation |
| **Complex unit (i)** | the lens's structural articulation of two-rooted self-reference |
| **Bootstrap moment** | the lens's verification that the seed is regenerated |

### How to use the template for a new lens

When designing a new experiment:

1. **Identify the lens's substrate** (its specific atomic content).
2. **Identify the lens's articulation** (its language for structural relations).
3. **For each template position, identify what fills it in this lens.** Some positions may be obvious; others may require deliberate construction.
4. **If a position cannot be filled**, that signals an incomplete articulation. The lens may need additional commitments to articulate that position, or the lens may not be a self-grounding lens of the framework.
5. **Verify the bootstrap**: in the articulation, check that the framework's seed is regenerated.

### What this changes for cross-domain analysis

Cross-domain transferability becomes structurally cleaner:

- **A forced result in one lens** identifies a structural position.
- **The "same" forced result in another lens** is whatever fills the same position there.
- **Convergences across lenses** are evidence that the position is structurally significant (not lens-specific).
- **Divergences** show where lens-specific commitments differ.

The algebraic package isn't just one experiment — it's the framework's canonical structural template. Every other experiment can be understood as filling this template with different content.
