# Atoms at Each Level

*Why first-principles thinking demands atomicity.*

---

## The principle

> **If X is not atomic, X is composite. Composites are by definition built from sub-parts. Working from first principles requires, at every level of articulation, identifying what is atomic — what cannot be further decomposed within the prior commitments at that level.**

This is not a choice — and the reason runs deeper than first-principles methodology. **Atomicity is structurally forced by [Internality](../01-postulates/internality.md) + [Self-Reference](../03-the-grammar/03-self-reference.md).**

The structural argument:

1. By Internality, no element of the totality can be accounted for from outside. Anything in the totality must be grounded internally.
2. Either an element is irreducible at its level (an atom), or it is composite of more-fundamental elements.
3. Composites must terminate at atoms — otherwise the recursion has no internal grounding (infinite descent with nothing standing under it would be external grounding, which Internality forbids).
4. By [Self-Reference as distinguishment](../03-the-grammar/03-self-reference.md#self-reference-as-distinguishment), polar pairs require a witness internal to the totality. Witnessing requires articulation. Articulation requires minimal forms to articulate from. **Without atoms at the level, there is nothing internal for the totality to articulate, so no structure can be internally grounded.**

The structural fact is **atemporal**: atoms and their composites coexist in the totality, and the requirement is that atoms *exist* at every level for internal grounding to obtain. There is no "before/after" at the framework level — time is itself a lens-level structure (see [arithmetic Topic 06](../10-experiments/mathematics/arithmetic/construction/06-iteration-and-time.md)).

What is sequenced ("trace atoms first, then articulate composites") belongs to *our* articulator's experience as runners-in-time, not to the framework's own structure. The empathic mode requires sequenced tracing because we exist in time; the framework's structural requirement holds simultaneously.

What *is* chosen is the **flavor of atomicity** — which sense of "indivisible" governs a level (algebraic, topological, logical, combinatorial, semantic). The seeking of atoms is forced; the kind of atom selected at a level is a flavor commitment per experiment.

## Why this matters

Without explicit atomicity, two failures occur:

1. **Stealth composites.** A "fundamental" structure turns out to be composite of unstated parts. The unstated parts do load-bearing work without being defended.
2. **Levels collapse.** What is atomic at one level is composite at another. Without level-explicit atomicity, arguments slide between scales without notice.

The atomicity principle prevents both: at every level, *here are the atoms, here is what is composite from them*.

## The levels of the generator

### Level 0 — The postulates (atoms of commitment)

The three [postulates](../01-postulates/) — Conservation, Existence, Internality — are atoms because:

- Removing any one collapses the framework to a degenerate position.
- None can be derived from the others or from anything more primitive.
- Each is a structurally distinct kind of constraint (negation, assertion, internalization).

### Level 1 — The grammar (atoms of standing structure)

The four standing properties in [the grammar](../03-the-grammar/) — Closure, Polarity, Self-Reference, Internality of Relations — are atoms because:

- Each is forced by a different combination of postulates.
- None can be derived from the others without engaging a postulate the others don't.
- Each describes a structurally distinct facet (boundedness, balance, self-observation, self-relating).

Any further "standing property" that is proposed is either composite of these four or requires an additional commitment beyond the postulates.

### Level n — Experiment commitments (atoms per commitment)

Each [experiment](../10-experiments/) introduces additional commitments. Each commitment introduces its own atoms at its own level. The experiment is responsible for naming them. Without that naming, the commitment is incomplete.

## How to identify atoms at a level

1. State the prior commitments at this level.
2. Identify a candidate atom.
3. Try to decompose it using only the prior commitments.
4. If no decomposition exists, the candidate is **atomic at this level**.
5. If a decomposition exists, the candidate is **composite**, and the decomposition reveals the actual atoms.

Honesty is in step 4: not stopping at the first decomposition that fails to come to mind.

## Atomicity is recursive

Each level's atoms become the building blocks of the next. The grammar's four properties are atoms relative to the postulates, but may be composite relative to a richer ontology that engages structures the postulates do not. An experiment introducing a "stability" commitment may decompose Polarity further; at the experiment's level, "stable polarity" might be the new atom.

This is fine. Atoms are atomic *at a level*.

## A bootstrapped package is itself an atom

When an experiment unfolds completely and verifies its [self-bootstrap](self-bootstrap.md) — articulating the framework's seed in its own vocabulary — the experiment-as-package becomes an atom at a higher level: the level of *self-grounding lenses of the generator*.

A self-grounding lens is composed of internal atoms (the topics in its construction chain), but as a whole, it is irreducible at the level above. It cannot be further decomposed into "more fundamental lenses" because it already contains the framework's full seed within itself.

This is meta-atomicity: the atomicity principle applied to packages. A package that bootstraps is atomic at the package-level even though it has internal structure.

What makes a package "forced" at this level:

- The choice of *which lens* (arithmetic vs. geometry vs. language) is itself a chosen move.
- Given the lens choice, the commitments made within the lens (e.g., C1–C6 in arithmetic) are chosen on minimality grounds — minimal for bootstrap.
- Given the commitments, the unfolding and the bootstrap-verification are forced.

So a self-bootstrapped package is *forced given its lens-choice and bootstrap-goal*. It is the unique self-grounding articulation of the framework through that lens.

This has cross-domain consequences (see [transferability](../15-cross-domain-analysis/transferability.md)): forced atoms within a bootstrapped package are forced atoms of the *generator*, articulated through that lens. They must transfer to other lenses that engage the same structural features.

## What this means for "minimality"

The framework's [forced-vs-chosen](forced-vs-chosen.md) discipline names "minimality principles" as one common kind of commitment. The atomicity principle clarifies what minimality actually does: it is a **flavor selector** for atomicity. Different minimality principles pick different atoms.

- Algebraic minimality: lowest-degree polynomial.
- Topological minimality: simplest manifold.
- Logical minimality: smallest axiom set.

Each is a flavor of atomicity, not a separate methodological move. The framework always seeks atoms; what an experiment chooses is *which kind*.

## What is not yet atomic

Three places where the framework currently lacks atomic articulation:

- **The reading of "sum" in $\Sigma = 0$.** Standard arithmetic addition is one possible atomic operation; alternatives exist. An experiment that needs a different sum-operation identifies a different atom here.
- **The notion of "element" of the totality.** The postulates don't specify what counts as an element. Experiments must commit.
- **The notion of "observation" in Existence.** The postulates give the structural role but not the atomic content of the observing-act.

Each is an invitation for explicit atomic articulation by experiments.
