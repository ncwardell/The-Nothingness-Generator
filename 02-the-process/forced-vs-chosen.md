# Forced vs. Chosen

*The discipline that keeps the generator honest.*

---

## The single most important habit

Every move in the unfolding is either **forced** or **chosen**:

- A **forced move** is one no alternative satisfies. Given the prior commitments, it is the unique resolution.
- A **chosen move** is one the generator could resolve in more than one way. The generator picks *this* resolution with stated reasons; other resolutions remain logically available.

Both are admissible. **Conflating them is not.** Calling a chosen move "forced" overstates the framework. Calling a forced move "chosen" understates it.

## How to tell which

For a candidate move from level $n$ to level $n+1$:

1. State the prior commitments at level $n$.
2. State the candidate move.
3. Try to construct an alternative that satisfies all prior commitments and produces a coherent level $n+1$.
4. If you cannot construct any alternative → **forced**.
5. If you can → **chosen**, and the selecting principle becomes a new explicit commitment.

This is mechanical when applied honestly. The discipline is in not stopping at the first failed alternative-attempt. A move that *feels* forced may merely *appear* so because alternatives are unfamiliar.

## The most common failure mode

The most common failure in structural metaphysics is to *recognize* a structure that fits and then declare it forced *at the wrong level*. The recognition is real — the structure does fit — but conflating framework-level forcing with lens-level forcing causes overclaim.

Two levels exist (see [framework-level vs lens-level forcing](../03-the-grammar/05-boundary.md#framework-level-vs-lens-level-forcing)):

- **Framework-level forced** — follows from the postulates alone, regardless of lens. Only the four standing properties qualify.
- **Lens-level forced** — follows from a lens's commitments + standing properties. Real, binding, articulable — but contingent on the lens.

The failure mode is claiming framework-level forcing when the actual forcing is lens-level. Each example below is **lens-level forced** but **not framework-level forced**:

- **"The minimum stable witnessing topology must be a directed three-cycle."** Forced *within the [topology experiment](../10-experiments/topology/topology.md)* once nodes/edges/directedness (T1) are committed and the stability requirement is imported from dynamics. Not forced at the framework level — without the topology lens, there is no triangle.
- **"Self-reference requires a Pythagorean form."** Forced *within the [math experiment's algebra subdomain](../10-experiments/mathematics/algebra/)* — the degenerate triple $i^2 + 1^2 = 0^2$ regenerates Conservation algebraically. Forced once A1 (algebraic articulation) is committed. Not forced at the framework level — without algebra, no Pythagorean form.
- **"Time emerges from iterated self-application."** Forced *within [arithmetic](../10-experiments/mathematics/arithmetic/construction/06-iteration-and-time.md)* via Self-Reference at the operational level once iteration is articulable. Not forced at the framework level — the framework alone doesn't articulate time.

Each is correctly forced within its lens. Honest articulation labels the level. Calling lens-level forcings "framework-level" overclaims; calling them "chosen" underclaims.

## What "minimality" actually means

Most chosen moves invoke some form of *minimality*. The framework's [atomicity principle](atoms.md) clarifies what this is: minimality is a **flavor selector** for atomicity. Different minimality principles select different atoms.

- **Algebraic minimality** — lowest-degree polynomial.
- **Topological minimality** — simplest non-trivial space.
- **Logical minimality** — smallest axiom set.
- **Information minimality** — shortest description.
- **Combinatorial minimality** — fewest elements.
- **Aesthetic minimality** — most symmetric, most elegant.

These are different selectors. They do not always agree. Invoking "minimality" without specifying which kind hides the actual commitment.

When a move is selected by minimality, name the kind. The atoms produced will be the atoms of *that flavor*.

## The boundary

The strict core in [`03-the-grammar/`](../03-the-grammar/) is what the postulates force at the **framework level** without minimality. The [boundary document](../03-the-grammar/05-boundary.md) marks where additional commitments must enter — and explicitly distinguishes framework-level from lens-level forcing.

Everything past the framework-level boundary is structure-plus-commitments. Within a lens, those commitments produce their own forced cascades (lens-level forcing). The reader is owed the explicit level at which any forcing claim operates: framework-wide (lens-independent), or within a specific lens (commitment-dependent).

## Why this discipline matters

A framework that maintains forced-vs-chosen is:

- **Defensible.** Skeptics can only reject the postulates or specific additional commitments. Both are clean targets.
- **Modular.** A specific commitment can be replaced and consequences re-traced, without disturbing the strict core.
- **Re-applicable.** The same generator can be applied to new domains with different commitments suited to them.

A framework that conflates is:

- **Brittle.** Any attack on a downstream structure threatens the whole edifice.
- **Closed.** Readers can't tell where to push back, extend, or substitute.
- **Easily dismissed.** The conflation reads as overclaiming; genuine content gets thrown out with the overclaim.

The generator survives serious scrutiny only if this discipline is maintained.
