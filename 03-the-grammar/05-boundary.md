# The Boundary of Strict Derivation

*Where the postulates stop forcing and any further articulation requires explicit commitments.*

---

## What the postulates strictly force at the framework level

From the three postulates alone, the standing properties of the totality are:

- [**Closure**](01-closure.md) — the totality has no exterior. (Restatement of Internality.)
- [**Polarity, weak form**](02-polarity.md) — non-zero contribution requires compensating contribution somewhere in the totality. (Conservation + Existence.)
- [**Self-Reference**](03-self-reference.md) — any internal observation references something internal; complete description includes itself. (Internality + Existence-as-observation.)
- [**Internality of Relations**](04-internality-of-relations.md) — relations between elements of the totality are themselves internal to the totality. (Direct corollary of Internality applied to relational structure.)

That is the entirety of the strictly-forced **framework-level** grammar — what holds of any totality satisfying the postulates, regardless of which lens articulates it.

## Framework-level vs lens-level forcing

The four standing properties above are forced **framework-wide**: they survive any experiment, any lens, any commitment chain. They are lens-independent.

Within a lens, the lens's commitments produce **additional forced consequences** — cascades of forced topics that follow once the lens admits its substrate and articulation. These lens-level forcings are real and binding *within the lens*, but they are contingent on the lens's commitments and do not appear at the framework level.

Examples of lens-level forced cascades:

- **Math experiment.** Once arithmetic admits binary polarity (C1) and addition as the binary form of $\Sigma$ (C3), the chain through Fibonacci → integers → self-reference equation → $\phi, \psi$ → degenerate triple is forced. None of those structures is forced framework-wide; all are forced once the math lens's commitments are in place.
- **Dynamics experiment.** Once the lens admits states and transitions over iteration, collapse pressure (unwitnessed polar pairs annihilate), two-node instability, and the minimum-cycle requirement are forced.
- **Topology experiment.** Once the lens admits nodes, edges, and directedness — and imports the stability requirement from dynamics — the triangle as minimum stable directed 3-cycle is forced; the double triangle is forced by polarity at the structural level; the four-structure via double-triangle bisection is forced.

Each experiment's chain documents its own lens-level forced cascade. The framework grammar (the four standing properties) does not absorb these — they remain lens-internal — but [cross-lens transferability](../15-cross-domain-analysis/transferability.md) connects them: a lens-level forced consequence that engages a structural feature shared with another lens is a candidate for finding the same consequence there in the other lens's vocabulary.

The honest separation:

- **Framework grammar** = forced framework-wide; survives any extension.
- **Lens forcings** = forced within a lens, contingent on the lens's commitments.
- **Cross-lens transferability** = how lens-level forcings propagate across experiments.

## What lies past the framework-level boundary

Any further articulation — algebraic, geometric, dynamical — requires **additional commitments** beyond the postulates. These are not in the postulates, and the postulates do not force them.

Common categories of additional commitments any experiment may need:

| Category | What it commits to |
|---|---|
| **Reading of "sum"** | The postulate $\Sigma = 0$ uses "sum" without defining it. Standard arithmetic addition is one reading; set-theoretic union, modal aggregation, or other operations are alternatives. Any specific reading is a commitment. |
| **Minimality principle** | Specifies which sense of "minimum" governs choices among logically-available alternatives (algebraic, topological, informational, combinatorial, aesthetic). |
| **Stability notion** | Defines what "persists" means and what counts as stable vs. unstable, when the postulates do not mention time or persistence. |
| **Specific algebraic form** | Selects a particular equation, polynomial form, or recurrence as the articulation of self-reference at the algebraic level. |
| **Specific topology** | Imposes a particular topological structure on the totality (a manifold, a discrete graph, a product space). |
| **Specific dynamics** | Imposes a particular notion of how the totality changes or re-enacts itself. |
| **Distinguishability rule** | Specifies how compensating contributions in weak polarity are individuated (single paired complement vs. distributed compensation, labeled vs. unlabeled). |
| **Metric or scale** | Imposes a notion of distance, magnitude, or measurement on the totality. |
| **Richness of structure** | Whether the totality is rich enough to support Tarski-style self-undescribability arguments (which require commitment to a specific level of structural complexity). |

Each of these, when added, extends the framework's articulation in a specific direction. None is forced by the three postulates alone.

## Why this matters

Two false positions become possible if the boundary is not respected:

- *Overclaim:* "The postulates entail this specific structure." False unless the structure is one of the three properties above.
- *Underclaim:* "The framework cannot speak to anything beyond the strict core." False — the framework speaks richly past the boundary, *given* explicit commitments. The commitments are what make the speaking possible.

The honest position is:

> **The framework is the strict grammar plus a specific set of additional commitments per experiment. The commitments are guided by reasoned principles (minimality, internality, structural elegance) but are not derivations. Productivity across domains is evidence that the commitments are well-chosen. It is not evidence that the commitments are forced.**

This sentence is the load-bearing organizational claim of the repo.

## How to cross the boundary

Crossing the boundary is the act of running [an experiment](../10-experiments/). To do so:

1. State the **phenomenon** the experiment will address.
2. State the **additional commitments** the experiment makes, explicitly and with reasoning.
3. Derive the consequences of the commitments under the [process](../02-the-process/dialectical-unfolding.md), honoring [forced-vs-chosen](../02-the-process/forced-vs-chosen.md) at every step.
4. Compare to the domain.

The boundary is not a wall. It is a customs checkpoint: anything passing through is named and labeled. Past the checkpoint, anything goes — provided its papers are in order.

## What survives in the strict grammar regardless

These properties hold of any totality satisfying the three postulates, no matter what additional commitments any experiment may add:

- The totality is closed.
- Non-zero contribution implies compensating contribution.
- Internal observations are self-referential.
- Relations between elements are internal to the totality.

If a proposed extension ever contradicts one of these, the extension is incompatible with the postulates, not with the experiment in particular. That is the diagnostic role of the strict grammar.
