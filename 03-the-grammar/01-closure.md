# Closure

> **The totality has no exterior.**

---

## Statement

There is no element outside the totality. The totality is, in the topological sense, closed and boundaryless.

## Derivation

Direct restatement of [Internality](../01-postulates/internality.md). The phrasing "no outside" and the phrasing "the totality is closed" refer to the same fact under different vocabularies. Closure is not a consequence of the postulate; it *is* the postulate, restated in the language of topology and reference.

## Status

**Definitional.** A direct restatement of Postulate 3 in different vocabulary.

## Why include it as a separate document

Two reasons:

1. The phrasing "the totality is closed" is the form in which the postulate is *used* in subsequent grammar and in any experiment. [Polarity](02-polarity.md) and [Self-Reference](03-self-reference.md) invoke closure as a named property. Naming it explicitly here clarifies what is being invoked.
2. Closure aligns the framework with topology, the right register for any later commitment that adds spatial or relational structure.

## What follows from Closure

Closure is invoked by:

- [Polarity](02-polarity.md) — the compensating contribution must be internal, because there is no external bookkeeping.
- [Self-Reference](03-self-reference.md) — observation must be internal, because there is no external observer.

## What does *not* follow from Closure

Closure does not, by itself, force any of the following without additional commitments:

- A specific topology on the totality (it could be any closed structure: discrete, continuous, or otherwise).
- A specific cardinality (finite, countable, or uncountable).
- A specific notion of "element" or what elements are.
- Any geometric structure.
- Any metric or distance.
- Any dynamics.

These require commitments listed at the [boundary](05-boundary.md).
