# The Grammar

*The atoms of standing structure. Standing properties of the totality, rigorously forced by the postulates.*

---

## What this section contains

The standing properties of the totality that follow strictly from the three [postulates](../01-postulates/) — Conservation, Existence, Internality — without any additional commitments. These are the framework's **rigorous content**: what is true of any totality satisfying the postulates, regardless of what further structure (algebraic, topological, dynamical) is later added.

The grammar is the **atoms of standing structure** at the postulate level — the irreducible facets of *what the totality must be like* given the postulates. (See [atoms at each level](../02-the-process/atoms.md) for what atomicity means here.) Each property in the grammar engages a different combination of postulates and cannot be reduced to the others without invoking a postulate that the others do not engage.

The grammar is what [the process](../02-the-process/) produces when run only on the postulates. It is the framework's standalone substance.

## Contents

1. **[Closure](01-closure.md)** — the totality has no exterior. (Direct restatement of Internality.)
2. **[Polarity (weak form)](02-polarity.md)** — non-zero contribution requires compensating contribution somewhere in the totality. (Conservation + Existence.)
3. **[Self-Reference](03-self-reference.md)** — every internal observation references something internal; complete description includes itself. (Internality + the observation reading of Existence.)
4. **[Internality of Relations](04-internality-of-relations.md)** — relations between elements of the totality are themselves internal to the totality. (Direct corollary of Internality applied to relational structure.)
5. **[The Boundary](05-boundary.md)** — explicit demarcation of where strict derivation ends and additional commitments must enter to continue.

These are the only standing properties strictly forced **at the framework level** by the postulates — they hold of any totality satisfying the postulates, regardless of lens. Everything richer requires explicit additional commitments and lives in [experiments](../10-experiments/), where the lens's commitments produce their own forced cascades (lens-level forcing). See [framework-level vs lens-level forcing](05-boundary.md#framework-level-vs-lens-level-forcing) for the two-level structure.

## Why the grammar is small

The three postulates are extremely thin. They commit you to a non-empty closed totality whose contents balance to zero, whose internal observations are self-referential, and whose internal relational structure is itself internal. That is all. The smallness is not a failure of the framework — it is the framework's strength. The discipline of articulating only what is forced makes the additional commitments visible *as commitments* whenever they are added.

## Synthesis: what the grammar amounts to

The four properties together describe a totality that is:

- **Bounded** (no outside) — Closure
- **Balanced** (compensation required) — Polarity
- **Self-observing** (internal observation, self-referential description) — Self-Reference
- **Self-relating** (relations are internal) — Internality of Relations

This is the framework's minimal picture of *being*: a self-contained, self-balancing, self-observing, self-relating totality. Any further articulation specifies *how* this totality is structured — which algebraic form, which topology, which dynamics — but the four properties above are what *any* totality satisfying the postulates must exhibit. They are the framework's standalone substance.

## What the grammar is good for

- **A defensible base.** The strict grammar is impervious to objections that target downstream commitments (e.g., a specific algebraic form, a specific topology). It is the smallest possible target for principled disagreement: only the postulates themselves.
- **A starting point for experiments.** Any [experiment](../10-experiments/) builds on the grammar by adding commitments. The grammar is the shared foundation across all experiments.
- **A test of internal coherence.** If the grammar were inconsistent with itself, the framework would fail before any experiment was run. It is not — the strictly-forced content is mutually consistent and structurally articulable.

## What the grammar is *not*

- Not a complete description of the framework. Most of the framework's substantive content lies past the boundary, in commitments and experiments.
- Not predictive of any specific phenomenon. The strict grammar is too thin to engage any specific domain on its own.
- Not the same as any specific experiment's grammar. Each experiment may layer additional grammar on top of this one (e.g., a specific algebraic structure that becomes the experiment's grammar). Those experiment-specific grammars are part of those experiments, not part of this section.
