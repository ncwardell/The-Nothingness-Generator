# 07. The Bisection

> **Algebraic assignment of the 6-element double triangle: 3 of the 6 elements are identified with the eigenvalues and unit $\{\phi, \psi, 1\}$; the polar partners give $\{-\phi, -\psi, -1\}$. The four-structure $\{\phi, \psi, 1, -1\}$ emerges as the named subset.**

## Commitment

**One commitment: the algebraic assignment.** Within chirality $\alpha$ on $\{A, B, C\}$, label the nodes such that:

- $A = \phi$ (one eigenvalue of self-reference)
- $B = \psi$ (the other eigenvalue)
- $C = 1$ (the unit; the Vieta sum $\phi + \psi$)

The polar partners in $\beta$ are then forced by additive polarity:

- $-A = -\phi$
- $-B = -\psi$
- $-C = -1$ (the additive polar of the unit; equivalently, the Vieta product $\phi\psi$)

The labeling within $\alpha$ is conventional — the cycle is structurally symmetric, so any of $A, B, C$ could play any of the roles. The commitment is the *choice to apply the math experiment's eigenvalue-and-unit labels* to topology's nodes; once that choice is made, the 6 elements have determinate algebraic identities.

## Construction

### The bisection

The 6-element structure with algebraic labels:

| Topological position | Algebraic value | Polar partner |
|---|---|---|
| $A$ (in $\alpha$) | $\phi$ | $-\phi$ (at $-A$) |
| $B$ (in $\alpha$) | $\psi$ | $-\psi$ (at $-B$) |
| $C$ (in $\alpha$) | $1$ | $-1$ (at $-C$) |
| $-A$ (in $\beta$) | $-\phi$ | $\phi$ (at $A$) |
| $-B$ (in $\beta$) | $-\psi$ | $\psi$ (at $B$) |
| $-C$ (in $\beta$) | $-1$ | $1$ (at $C$) |

The 6 elements algebraically: $\{\phi, \psi, 1, -\phi, -\psi, -1\}$.

### The four-structure

The four-structure $\{\phi, \psi, 1, -1\}$ is the subset:

$$\{A, B, C, -C\} = \{\phi, \psi, 1, -1\}$$

Three from $\alpha$ (the eigenvalues $\phi, \psi$ and the unit $1$) plus one from $\beta$ (the polar partner of the unit, $-1$).

The remaining 2 topological elements, $-A = -\phi$ and $-B = -\psi$, are forced by additive polarity but are not in the named four-structure. They are the *derivable* elements — recoverable from the four-structure via the negation map.

This matches the math experiment's articulation: in [algebra](../../mathematics/algebra/), the four-structure $\{\phi, \psi, 1, -1\}$ is what Vieta produces; $-\phi$ and $-\psi$ are forced by polarity but not separately named.

### The cycle as Vieta's relations

The chirality $\alpha$ cycle, $A \to B \to C \to A$, becomes algebraically $\phi \to \psi \to 1 \to \phi$. The cycle's three steps encode structural relations among the three values:

- $\phi \to \psi$: from one eigenvalue to the other. Algebraically: $\psi = -1/\phi$ (multiplicative-polar) or $\psi = 1 - \phi$ (additive complement to unity).
- $\psi \to 1$: from the second eigenvalue to the unit. Algebraically: $1 = \phi + \psi$ (Vieta sum).
- $1 \to \phi$: from the unit to the first eigenvalue. Algebraically: $\phi = 1 - \psi$ (subtraction of the other eigenvalue from the unit).

The cycle traverses the Vieta relations: each step relates two of the four-structure's primary elements.

In $\beta$, the anti-chiral cycle $-A \to -C \to -B \to -A$ traverses the negated Vieta relations on $\{-\phi, -\psi, -1\}$.

### The polar matching as Conservation

The 3 polar matching relations $\{(A, -A), (B, -B), (C, -C)\} = \{(\phi, -\phi), (\psi, -\psi), (1, -1)\}$ encode additive Conservation:

- $\phi + (-\phi) = 0$
- $\psi + (-\psi) = 0$
- $1 + (-1) = 0$

Each polar pair sums to zero. Conservation is articulated at the polar-matching level.

- **Polarity:** ✓ — both at the value level (additive polar pairs) and at the chirality level (anti-chiral cycles). The four-structure has both senses: $\{1, -1\}$ additive polar; $\{\phi, \psi\}$ multiplicatively polar (since $\phi\psi = -1$).
- **Closure:** ✓ — the cycle structure closes; the polar matching is internal; all algebraic relations are within the value system $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 5)$ that the math experiment articulates.
- **Self-Reference:** ✓ — the eigenvalues $\phi, \psi$ are the eigenvalues of self-reference; the cycle structure encodes the Vieta relations among them.
- **Internality of Relations:** ✓ — all algebraic relations (sum, product, polar) are internal to the totality.

## Comparison

The math experiment's algebra subdomain produces $\{\phi, \psi, 1, -1\}$ via Vieta on the self-reference equation $x^2 = x + 1$. The topology experiment produces the same four-structure via the bisection of the 6-element double triangle.

These are **two genuinely independent commitment chains** arriving at the same forced atom:

- **Math path:** arithmetic substrate → algebraic articulation (A1) → self-reference equation → roots $\phi, \psi$ → Vieta relations → four-structure.
- **Topology path:** topology substrate (T1) + connectivity (T2) → directed 3-cycle → chirality → double triangle → 6 elements (Polarity at node level) → bisection (algebraic assignment) → four-structure.

The convergence is at the four-structure level: same 4 elements, same polar relationships ($\{1, -1\}$ additive, $\{\phi, \psi\}$ multiplicative).

The framework's [transferability principle](../../../15-cross-domain-analysis/transferability.md) treats this kind of cross-lens convergence as evidence that the four-structure is a forced atom of the *generator*, not lens-specific. Two paths converging on the same structure with non-shared commitment chains is the strongest form of cross-domain evidence.
