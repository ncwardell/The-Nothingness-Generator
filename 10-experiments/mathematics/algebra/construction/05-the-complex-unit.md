# 05. The Complex Unit

> **The geometric mean of the two eigenvalues: $\sqrt{\phi\psi} = \sqrt{-1} = i$. The complex unit emerges, forcing extension to complex numbers.**

---

## Question

Vieta gives $\phi\psi = -1$. The geometric mean of two values $a, b$ is $\sqrt{a \cdot b}$. *What is the geometric mean of $\phi$ and $\psi$?*

## Dependencies

- [03. Vieta's relations](03-vietas-relations.md) — $\phi\psi = -1$.
- [02. The two roots](02-the-two-roots.md) — $\phi$ and $\psi$ exist.

## Commitment

None. The complex unit is forced by the algebraic structure.

## Construction

From Vieta: $\phi \cdot \psi = -1$.

Take the square root (using the multiplicative operation from arithmetic):

$$\sqrt{\phi \cdot \psi} = \sqrt{-1}$$

By definition, $\sqrt{-1} = i$, the imaginary unit. So:

$$i = \sqrt{\phi \psi}$$

The complex unit $i$ is the **geometric mean of the two eigenvalues of self-reference**.

## What this articulates

The two-rooted structure of self-reference forces extension to complex numbers:

- The polar pair $\{\phi, \psi\}$ has product $-1$.
- Their geometric mean must be a value $v$ with $v^2 = -1$.
- No real $v$ satisfies this. Closure forces internal extension.
- The extension is the complex numbers $\mathbb{C}$, with $i$ as the new generator.

$i$ is not posited; it is forced by the algebraic structure of self-reference. Complex numbers are not an external addition to arithmetic — they are a forced consequence of self-reference admitting two roots whose product is negative.

## All four standing properties respected

- **Conservation:** $i^2 + 1 = 0$ — the complex unit and the unit of distinction sum (with squaring) to zero. This is another form of the degenerate triple from topic 04.
- **Polarity:** $i$ has its polar partner $-i$. The complex unit comes with its negation, by polarity.
- **Closure:** the value system extends to $\mathbb{C}$ to admit $i$. Closure is preserved (the extension is internal — driven by the algebra, not externally posited).
- **Self-Reference:** $i$ is the geometric mean of the two eigenvalues of self-reference; it is forced by the two-fold structure of self-reference.
- **Internality of Relations:** $i$ is defined internally by $i^2 = -1$.

## Comparison

In standard mathematics, $i$ is introduced by various routes (stipulation, algebraic closure of $\mathbb{R}$, geometric rotation). The framework's contribution: $i$ is the geometric mean of the two eigenvalues whose product is $-1$ — its structural origin is the two-rooted nature of self-reference.
