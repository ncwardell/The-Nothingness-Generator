# 10. Zeckendorf Composition

> **Every positive integer has a unique representation as a sum of non-consecutive Fibonacci numbers. The integers are generated as compositions of Fibonacci atoms.**

---

## Question

Fibonacci (topic 08) and NegaFibonacci (topic 09) generate an infinite set of atomic values. But many positive integers are not themselves Fibonacci numbers (4, 6, 7, 9, ...). *How are these integers admitted to the value system without violating Closure?*

## Dependencies

- [08. Fibonacci](08-fibonacci.md) — the Fibonacci sequence generates atoms.
- [05. Addition and subtraction](05-addition-and-subtraction.md) — composition is by addition.

## Commitment

None new. Zeckendorf composition is a forced consequence of Fibonacci + addition under the Closure requirement.

## Construction

Fibonacci generates an infinite sequence of atomic values: $F(2) = 1, F(3) = 2, F(4) = 3, F(5) = 5, F(6) = 8, \ldots$ (excluding $F(0) = 0$ and $F(1) = 1$ as redundant; $F(2) = 1$ suffices).

Most positive integers are not Fibonacci numbers. To admit them without violating [Closure](../../../../03-the-grammar/01-closure.md) (no external posit), they must be generated *internally* — by composition of Fibonacci atoms via addition.

**Zeckendorf's theorem:** every positive integer has a unique representation as a sum of non-consecutive Fibonacci numbers.

Examples:

| Integer | Zeckendorf representation | Atoms used |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $F(2)$ | $\{1\}$ |
| 2 | $F(3)$ | $\{2\}$ |
| 3 | $F(4)$ | $\{3\}$ |
| 4 | $F(4) + F(2)$ | $\{3, 1\}$ |
| 5 | $F(5)$ | $\{5\}$ |
| 6 | $F(5) + F(2)$ | $\{5, 1\}$ |
| 7 | $F(5) + F(3)$ | $\{5, 2\}$ |
| 8 | $F(6)$ | $\{8\}$ |
| 11 | $F(6) + F(4)$ | $\{8, 3\}$ |
| 12 | $F(6) + F(4) + F(2)$ | $\{8, 3, 1\}$ |
| 17 | $F(7) + F(4) + F(2)$ | $\{13, 3, 1\}$ |
| 100 | $F(11) + F(6) + F(4)$ | $\{89, 8, 3\}$ |

The "non-consecutive" constraint forces uniqueness.

Each positive integer is therefore a unique *Zeckendorf word* — a structural fingerprint of how it composes from self-referential atoms.

**Why uniqueness is forced, not chosen.** Mathematically, the non-consecutive rule is what makes Zeckendorf's theorem give a *unique* decomposition; this is a theorem about positive integers and Fibonacci numbers. Structurally, the uniqueness is forced by [Internality of Relations](../../../../03-the-grammar/04-internality-of-relations.md): the relation between an integer and its atomic decomposition must itself be internal and well-defined. A one-to-many relation would require an external choice of representation to disambiguate — externalization through choice is forbidden. A canonical decomposition is what Internality of Relations requires; the non-consecutive rule is what produces it.

## What this gives

- **The positive integers $\mathbb{Z}^+$** are in the value system, generated rather than posited.
- **Each integer has a structural identity** — its Zeckendorf decomposition.

## All four standing properties respected

- **Conservation:** composition by addition preserves $\Sigma = 0$.
- **Polarity:** every positive integer admitted has its polar complement (forced — addressed in topic 11).
- **Closure:** ✓ — central point. Each integer is generated by addition of atoms already in the value system. No external posit.
- **Self-Reference:** atoms are self-referentially generated; composition uses internal operations.
- **Internality of Relations:** composition relations are internal.

## Comparison

In standard mathematics, Zeckendorf's theorem is well-known. The framework's contribution: Zeckendorf composition is the *unique Closure-respecting integer generator at the postulate level*. Successor (Peano) violates Closure by externally positing "the next integer"; Fibonacci+Zeckendorf does not.

The integers are not points on a pre-existing line; they are unique compositions of self-referential atoms.

## Transferability

**Engages:** Closure + Self-Reference + Polarity + iteration.

**Prediction:** Any lens admitting an extended value system from self-referentially-generated atoms articulates an analogous unique-composition theorem.

## What this enables

The positive integers are in place. Polarity forces their negative complements. That is [11. Negative integers](11-negative-integers.md).
