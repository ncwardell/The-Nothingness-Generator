# 02. Zero

> **Zero is the value of the conservative sum over the polar pair.**

---

## Question

The polar pair $\{E, -E\}$ exists (topic 01). Conservation says $\Sigma = 0$. *What value does the conservative sum produce when applied to the polar pair?*

## Dependencies

- [01. Polar bisection](01-polar-bisection.md) — the polar pair $\{E, -E\}$ exists.

## Commitment

None. Zero is forced.

## Construction

By [Conservation](../../../../01-postulates/conservation.md), the conservative sum $\Sigma$ over the totality is zero. Restricted to the polar pair:

$$\Sigma_{\{E, -E\}} = 0$$

The "$0$" on the right is the value the conservative sum produces. It is forced — not committed; it is what Conservation says directly.

Zero is therefore not a separate posit. It is a value: what $\Sigma$ produces over the polar pair, and (by extension) over the totality.

A note: $0$ is a *value*, not necessarily an *element*. The postulate forces the *sum* to be zero; whether $0$ appears as an element of the totality is downstream.

## All four standing properties respected

- **Conservation:** $\Sigma = 0$ is the postulate; this topic only names what it produces.
- **Polarity:** the polar pair sums to zero — the conservative reference between the poles.
- **Closure:** zero is in the value system as a forced value, not as an external posit.
- **Self-Reference:** $\Sigma$ is the operation by which the totality sums itself; zero is the value of this self-sum.
- **Internality of Relations:** the relation "$\Sigma = 0$" is the postulate, internal by definition.

## Comparison

In standard mathematics, $0$ is the additive identity. Here, $0$ is generated as the value of the conservative sum, *before* binary addition is articulated. The additive-identity role is recovered later when addition is committed.

## Transferability

**Engages:** Conservation + Polarity.

**Prediction:** Any lens engaging Conservation + Polarity articulates a "conservative reference value" — the value the polar pair sums to.
